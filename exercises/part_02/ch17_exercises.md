# Chapter 17 Exercises: Shape, Skewness, and Outliers

1. Sketch a symmetric, a right-skewed, and a left-skewed distribution.
2. In a right-skewed distribution, which direction contains the longer tail?
3. Explain why right skew does not mean that most observations are large.
4. For `1, 2, 3, 4, 5`, calculate Bowley skewness.
5. For `1, 2, 3, 4, 20`, calculate Q1, Q3, IQR, and the conventional 1.5-IQR fences.
6. Which observation is flagged in Question 5?
7. Explain why a flagged observation is not automatically a data error.
8. Give one legitimate operational reason for an unusually long NRG delivery.
9. Give one data-quality reason for an unusually long recorded delivery.
10. Compare standard z-scores with median/MAD-based robust scores.
11. Why can ordinary boxplot fences overflag observations in a skewed distribution?
12. Describe a bimodal business distribution and what might create its two peaks.
13. Explain why deleting flagged values can bias a service-level analysis.
14. State an appropriate treatment for a confirmed unit-entry error.
15. State an appropriate treatment for a rare but valid disruption.
16. Write a short outlier-investigation checklist that preserves an audit trail.
