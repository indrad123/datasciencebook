# Chapter 19 Exercises: Sampling Distributions and the Central Limit Theorem

1. Distinguish a population distribution, sample distribution, and sampling distribution.
2. Explain why one sample cannot display its own sampling distribution.
3. If population mean is 100, what is the expected sample mean?
4. If population SD is 20 and `n=25`, calculate the standard error of the mean.
5. Recalculate Question 4 for `n=100` and explain the change.
6. What sample-size multiplication is needed to halve standard error?
7. State the central limit theorem for independent identically distributed observations with finite variance.
8. Does the CLT say that raw observations become normally distributed? Explain.
9. Does `n=30` guarantee an adequate normal approximation? Explain.
10. Why might a very skewed population require a larger sample?
11. Explain how cluster dependence affects the usual standard-error formula.
12. For `N=100` and `n=10`, calculate the finite population correction.
13. When is that correction relevant?
14. Explain the difference between standard deviation and standard error.
15. Describe a repeated-sampling simulation for NRG store sales.
16. List assumptions and diagnostics to check before using a CLT-based approximation.
