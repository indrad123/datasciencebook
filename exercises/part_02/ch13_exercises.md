# Chapter 13 Exercises

1. Define an observational unit in your own words.
2. In a table with one row per retail store, name two possible variables.
3. For a study of all NRG retail stores operating in Indonesia in August 2026, state the population.
4. Distinguish the target population from the sampling frame.
5. Explain why a sample is not simply a smaller population with guaranteed identical characteristics.
6. Classify “store region” as an identifier, categorical variable, or quantitative variable.
7. Classify “weekly revenue in IDR” as an identifier, categorical variable, or quantitative variable.
8. Explain why a customer ID should not normally be averaged.
9. Distinguish a parameter from a statistic.
10. A population contains weekly sales of 80, 100, 120, 140, and 160 units. Find the population mean.
11. A sample contains 80, 120, and 160 units. Find its mean and its estimation error relative to Exercise 10.
12. Find the sampling fraction when 50 stores are selected from 400 stores.
13. Describe one reason a complete census can still contain error.
14. Explain why a large convenience sample can be less useful than a smaller probability sample.
15. State the unit of analysis for a dataset with one row per store-month.
16. Challenge: NRG wants to estimate average delivery time for all August orders, but its frame excludes telephone orders. Identify the coverage problem and describe the population to which the result can safely apply.
