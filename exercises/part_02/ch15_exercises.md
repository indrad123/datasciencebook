# Chapter 15 Exercises

1. Distinguish a count from a relative frequency.
2. Construct a frequency table for `tea, coffee, tea, spice, coffee, tea`.
3. What should the relative frequencies in a complete frequency table sum to?
4. Explain the purpose of a contingency table.
5. Choose a chart for comparing sales totals across product categories and justify it.
6. Choose a chart for showing the distribution of 500 delivery durations.
7. Explain why bars in a histogram touch while bars in a categorical bar chart are normally separated.
8. Explain how changing histogram bin width can change the visible pattern.
9. State one appropriate use for a line chart.
10. Explain why connecting unordered product categories with a line can mislead.
11. What does an empirical cumulative distribution function show at a value of 4 days?
12. Explain why a truncated vertical axis can exaggerate a bar comparison.
13. State two reasons to prefer direct position or length comparisons over pie-slice comparisons.
14. Explain why a chart should display units and the population or period represented.
15. Distinguish a distribution from a list of raw observations.
16. Challenge: Design a compact three-view NRG report for product mix, delivery-time distribution, and daily sales trend. Name each table or chart and explain what question it answers.
