5. Storage describes computer representation. Measurement scale depends on what values mean and which comparisons or arithmetic operations are valid.
6. NRG collecting a new customer survey is primary collection. Reusing point-of-sale transactions created for operations is secondary use for the new analytical question.
7. Advantage: large, timely records generated during operations. Risk: definitions, coverage, and missingness reflect the operational system rather than the research question.
8. Every eligible unit has a known and equal chance of selection under the stated design.
9. Choose a random start in the first ten orders, then select approximately every tenth order through the frame.
10. When important subgroups must be represented or differ substantially on the outcome, samples can be drawn within each subgroup.
11. Sampling whole locations or groups reduces travel and administration, but units within one cluster may share conditions and provide less independent information.
12. Sampling error is variation from observing a subset. Coverage error occurs when the frame does not properly include the target population.
13. Random error varies unpredictably around the reference value. Systematic bias shifts measurements consistently in a direction or pattern.
14. Errors are 0.3, 0.4, and 0.2 kg. Average bias is \((0.3+0.4+0.2)/3=0.3\) kg.
15. Nonresponse: selected customers do not answer. Processing: an import converts decimal commas incorrectly or duplicates rows.
16. Voluntary participation creates self-selection, app-only coverage excludes offline customers, and the leading wording creates measurement error. Mitigations include probability-based recruitment across channels, a frame that covers offline customers, neutral tested wording, and follow-up or weighting for documented nonresponse.
