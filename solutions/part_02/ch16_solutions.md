# Chapter 16 Solutions: Centres, Quantiles, and Spread

1. `(2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 5) / 5 = 3` days.
2. The middle ordered value is 3 days.
3. The modes are 2 and 3 days because each occurs twice.
4. `5 - 2 = 3` days.
5. Q1 is 2, Q3 is 3, and the IQR is 1 day.
6. Squared deviations from 3 sum to 6. Sample variance is `6 / 4 = 1.5` days squared; sample standard deviation is about 1.225 days.
7. The new mean is 7.5 days and the median is 3 days. The mean changes much more because the extreme value enters its sum directly.
8. Both resist the influence of a small number of extreme observations and together describe a typical position and the middle half of the data.
9. `(120 x 40 + 150 x 10) / 50 = 126` units.
10. An unweighted average would treat a 40-store region and a 10-store region as equally large.
11. The position is `3 x 0.25 = 0.75`, so the result is `10 + 0.75(20 - 10) = 17.5`.
12. Sample quantiles require a convention for locating or interpolating between ordered observations. Packages may use different conventions.
13. The standard deviation is expressed in days and measures typical squared-deviation-based spread around the mean. It is not a maximum distance or a guarantee about individual deliveries.
14. The store with the larger standard deviation has less predictable daily demand, which may require more safety stock or flexible staffing.
15. The range depends only on two extreme observations. Standard deviation is sensitive to extreme values and can be misleading when reported alone for a skewed distribution.
16. Report the median and IQR, plus selected percentiles or a distribution plot. These describe the centre and middle spread without allowing a few very large purchases to dominate.
