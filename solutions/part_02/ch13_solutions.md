# Chapter 13 Solutions

1. The observational unit is the entity or case about which one row or record provides information.
2. Examples include region, floor area, weekly revenue, or customer count.
3. Every NRG retail store operating in Indonesia during the stated period.
4. The target population is the group the study seeks to understand. The sampling frame is the operational list from which units can actually be selected.
5. Random selection produces variation. Sample summaries can differ from population parameters, even under a sound design.
6. Categorical variable.
7. Quantitative variable.
8. The numeric-looking values label customers; their arithmetic magnitude has no substantive meaning.
9. A parameter describes a population. A statistic is calculated from a sample and may estimate a parameter.
10. \((80+100+120+140+160)/5=120\) units.
11. The sample mean is \((80+120+160)/3=120\), so its estimation error is zero for this sample.
12. \(50/400=0.125\), or 12.5%.
13. A census can have missing responses, duplicate records, measurement mistakes, processing errors, or an incorrectly defined population.
14. More observations do not repair systematic exclusion or self-selection. Probability sampling makes the selection mechanism explicit and can better support population inference.
15. A store-month, not merely a store or a month.
16. Telephone orders have undercoverage because they are absent from the frame. Without adjustment or another frame, the result safely describes only August orders represented in the available ordering system.
