# Appendix C Solutions: Mathematical Symbols and Notation

1. $=$ asserts equality; $\approx$ states that values are close under an approximation or rounding rule.
2. $x$ belongs to the interval from 0 inclusive to 1 exclusive.
3. $A\cap B=\{3\}$, $A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4\}$, and $A\setminus B=\{1,2\}$.
4. $2(1)+2(2)+2(3)+2(4)=20$.
5. $\bar{x}=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=1}^{5}x_i$.
6. Mathematical sequences commonly label the first item 1; Python uses zero-based positions, so mathematical $x_1$ is often `x[0]`.
7. $f(g(3))=f(9)=19$.
8. $\mathbf{X}$ has shape $100\times5$, $\mathbf{w}$ has five components, and $\mathbf{Xw}$ has 100 components.
9. $1(4)+2(0)+3(-1)=1$.
10. $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$.
11. $\Delta y/\Delta x$ is an average change over a finite interval; $dy/dx$ is the limiting instantaneous rate where it exists.
12. It is the probability of $A$ given $B$; the bar introduces the conditioning information.
13. $Y$ is a random variable, $y$ an observed or possible value, $\mu$ a population parameter, and $\hat\mu$ its estimate.
14. $18/24=0.75$.
15. It returns a value of $\theta$ that minimises $L$, not the minimum loss value.
16. The observation frequency and ordering must be known. It could mean seven days, weeks, transactions, or another unit.
17. For every $x$ in set $A$, $f(x)$ is non-negative.
18. Cases and cases per day are incompatible for addition. The rate must first be multiplied by a duration, or the count divided by one.
19. `sum(weight * value for weight, value in zip(w, x))`. Validate equal lengths and finite numeric values.
20. Example: $\mathbf{X}$ is the feature matrix available at origin $T$; $y_t$ is observed weekly demand at week $t$; $\hat y_{t+h}$ is the forecast for horizon $h$; $T$ is the last observation available when forecasting; and $h$ is weeks ahead.
