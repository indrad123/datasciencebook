# Chapter 47 Solutions

1. Discrimination ranks cases; calibration aligns predicted probabilities with observed frequencies.
2. About 70% should experience the event over the stated horizon in comparable future data.
3. Average the squared differences between each probability and binary outcome.
4. Thresholded labels discard probability quality and can remain unchanged after severe probability distortion.
5. The intercept detects systematic over- or underprediction; the slope detects probabilities that are too extreme or too narrow.
6. Use it on held-out data when a sigmoid transformation is plausible.
7. A flexible step function can overfit small calibration samples.
8. Reusing training cases leaks fitted optimism into recalibration.
9. Include baseline, discrimination, calibration, proper scores, cost, subgroup results, latency, and uncertainty.
10. Global explanations summarize model behavior; local explanations describe one prediction relative to a reference.
11. Shuffle a feature and measure the loss of predictive performance.
12. A correlated substitute may preserve performance, splitting or concealing importance.
13. It is a model-attribution relative to a background distribution, not an inherent property of the person or process.
14. Predictive dependence can reflect confounding, proxies, and model structure rather than intervention effects.
15. Repeat across samples and plausible background populations, then inspect rank and sign stability.
16. Record audience, question, method, reference set, validation, limitations, prohibited interpretations, and escalation path.
